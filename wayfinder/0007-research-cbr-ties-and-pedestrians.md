## Question

Three open points from the rules research ([research/dutch-priority-rules.md](research/dutch-priority-rules.md)) that the exam's expected answers decide:
1. In the CBR sleepvraag (numbering road users 1-2-3), can two road users share a number (simultaneous moves), or is the order always strict? Check CBR example animations, official practice exams, and examenplan wording.
2. When turning into a side road, what answer does the CBR expect for pedestrians crossing that side road (art. 18 "verkeer" vs CBR's "no legal priority for pedestrians at junctions")?
3. Roundabout cyclists: is "cyclists on the cycle ring have priority inside built-up areas only" ever law, or purely signage/design (CROW)? What does the exam assume when signs are shown?

## Resolution

- **Ties (Q1):** No. Treat the sleepvraag order as strict, with each number used once. CBR's wording is "eerste … volgende … laatste", and CBR's own demo GIF offers exactly one token per number (1/2/3 for 3 road users), each consumed when placed. Ties are a non-CBR app extension. Confidence: medium-high. No official answer key or practice exam exists to confirm it.
- **Pedestrians when turning (Q2):** Let them go. Under art. 18(1) the turner must "voor laten gaan" all "verkeer" (pedestrians included) going straight on along the same road. CBR's "no voorrang for pedestrians at junctions" is about *voorrang* (owed only to bestuurders), so the two do not conflict. CBR-plan lists "voor laten gaan van voetgangers" as exam material. Pedestrians elsewhere yield unless a zebra or uitrit applies. Confidence: medium (law clear; exam answer inferred).
- **Roundabout cyclists (Q3):** Never law. "In de voorrang binnen de bebouwde kom" is a non-binding CROW recommendation (minister to Tweede Kamer, 2009), and the RVV 1990 drafting notes say priority on roundabouts must be set with signs. In the exam, the drawn haaientanden or B6 decide. Confidence: high (law), medium-high (exam assumption).
