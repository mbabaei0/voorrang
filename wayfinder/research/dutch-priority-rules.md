## 1. The exam: what is tested and in what form

### 1.1 Format (since 7 April 2025)

- 50 scored questions plus 2 unscored test questions that look the same as the others. Pass mark is **44 of 50**. Time is 30 minutes (45 with extra time). There are 2 practice questions before the exam starts. (CBR-how, CBR-plan)
- The old three-part exam (hazard perception 25, knowledge 12, insight 28, each with its own pass mark) was merged into one. The hazard-perception photo test was dropped. **Short animations without sound** were added: they test perceiving and predicting ("what happens next / how do you act"). (CBR-news)
- Assumptions every answer rests on (CBR-plan, "Aandachtspunten"): **you are the driver of the CBR car**; **other road users follow the rules** (including speed limits); road and weather conditions are normal, *unless the picture or question clearly says otherwise*.

### 1.2 Topic list (8 onderwerpen) (CBR-topics, CBR-plan)

1. Gebruik van de weg (K+)
2. **Voorrang en voor laten gaan**: giving voorrang at junctions, voor laten gaan when turning, voor laten gaan of pedestrians (K+)
3. Bijzondere wegen, weggedeelten, weggebruikers en manoeuvres: motorways, **roundabouts** (in the examenplan) or **erven** (on the topics page), military columns, special manoeuvres (K+)
4. Veilig rijden met het voertuig en reageren in noodsituaties (K+)
5. **Verkeerstekens en aanwijzingen**: signs, lights, road markings, instructions from police officers and others (K+)
6. Verantwoorde verkeersdeelname en milieubewust rijden (K+)
7. Wetgeving (K)
8. Voertuigkennis (K)

Levels: **K** = name or recognise a fact or rule (CBR's example: what does this sign mean?). **K+** = take the right action in a traffic situation and show you understand the rule. CBR's own K+ example is: *"Wat is de juiste volgorde van voor laten gaan?"* That is exactly the app's core puzzle. (CBR-plan)

Legal scope of the exam (Ministeriële regeling eisen theorie-examen B, art. 1, as reproduced in CBR-plan): **RVV 1990 arts. 1–98 plus Bijlagen 1 and 2** (so all signs *and* the traffic-controller gestures), BABW arts. 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, WVW arts. incl. 5, and others not relevant to priority.

### 1.3 Question formats (CBR-types)

- **Sleepvraag** (drag question): drag numbers onto road users in the picture. The first to go gets 1, the next 2, the last 3. **This is the native exam format for passing-order questions.**
- Single-sleepvraag: drag a check mark to one place in the picture.
- Invulvraag: type a number.
- Hotspotvraag: tap the right spot in the picture.
- Yes/no and multiple choice.
- Multiple response: more than one answer can be correct.
- Plus the 2025 animations (CBR-news).

Not stated by CBR: whether a sleepvraag ever accepts the **same number for two road users** (simultaneous moves). CBR's wording ("1, de volgende 2, de laatste 3") implies a strict order. See §6.

Vocabulary: CBR does **not translate "erf"** (also not "autoweg", "brommobiel", "snorfiets", "speed pedelec", "APK") in English-language exams. (CBR-plan §6) This matters for the app's EN text.

## 2. Two different duties: "voorrang verlenen" vs "voor laten gaan"

The RVV uses two terms, and the CBR keeps them apart (RP-B p.72).

- **Voorrang verlenen** (to give priority): defined as "het de betrokken bestuurders in staat stellen ongehinderd hun weg te vervolgen" (RVV art. 1). It is owed only to **bestuurders**, meaning all road users except pedestrians (RVV art. 1, "bestuurders: alle weggebruikers behalve voetgangers"). Used in arts. 15, 80 and signs B6/B7.
- **Voor laten gaan** (to let go first): owed to "verkeer" or named groups. **Verkeer** means *all* road users, including pedestrians (RVV art. 1). Used in arts. 15a, 18, 49, 50, 54.

Consequence (RP-B p.27, p.44, p.72): at a junction a driver legally owes pedestrians no *voorrang*. But in an **uitrit / special manoeuvre** (art. 54) the driver must let pedestrians go too, and at a **zebra** (art. 49(2)) pedestrians must be let go.

Road users who count as **pedestrians** (RVV art. 2): people walking a bike, moped or motorbike by hand; people on "voorwerpen, niet zijnde voertuigen" (e.g. skates, steps); disabled-vehicle users on a pavement or crossing from pavement to pavement. A **snorfiets** is a bicycle for these rules (art. 2b). A **brommobiel** is a motor vehicle (art. 2a).

## 3. Precedence hierarchy

| Rank | What | Article |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aanwijzingen** (instructions by gesture or voice) from authorised, recognisable officials, KMar, authorised verkeersregelaars, and verkeersregelaars in training. Also the stop sign of a **verkeersbrigadier** (Bijlage II onderdeel 8), and a stop sign from a rail-vehicle escort (model F10 sign, red flag, red lamp). | RVV 82, 82a, 83; **84: "Aanwijzingen gaan boven verkeerstekens en verkeersregels."** |
| 2 | **Verkeerslichten** over priority signs | **RVV 64: "Verkeerslichten gaan boven verkeerstekens die de voorrang regelen."** |
| 3 | **Verkeerstekens** (signs and road markings) over rules, where they conflict. Temporary road markings beat permanent ones. | **RVV 63: "Verkeerstekens gaan boven verkeersregels, voor zover deze regels onverenigbaar zijn met deze tekens."** 63a. BABW 3 says verkeerstekens = signs, lights and road markings. |
| 4 | **Verkeersregels** (general rules: right-hand rule, turning, special manoeuvres, etc.) | RVV ch. II |

**Emergency vehicles (voorrangsvoertuigen)** fall outside this ladder. RVV 50: "Weggebruikers moeten bestuurders van een voorrangsvoertuig voor laten gaan." That is a *rule*, so in principle signs and lights rank above it (art. 63). But art. 63 only applies where rule and sign are *onverenigbaar*. A green light means "doorgaan" (art. 68). It permits you to go and does not stop you yielding, so no conflict arises **(interpretation)**. Also, voorrangsvoertuigen may depart from all RVV rules "voor zover de uitoefening van hun taak dit vereist" (RVV 91), so they may run a red light. Practical exam model: **yield to a voorrangsvoertuig in every situation. Only an aanwijzing from a traffic controller beats that** **(interpretation)**, consistent with RP-B p.23 and p.29, which list voorrangsvoertuigen first among those you must always let go.

Safety override (RP-B p.18): where signs or rules conflict with each other, follow whichever serves road safety most. If breaking a rule prevents an accident, break it. Also WVW art. 5. Exam questions assume others obey the rules (CBR-plan), so this override rarely applies in puzzles.

## 4. Rules by topic

### 4.1 Unmarked (gelijkwaardige) junctions: right-hand rule

**RVV 15**
> 1 Op kruispunten verlenen bestuurders voorrang aan voor hen van rechts komende bestuurders.
> 2 Op deze regel gelden de volgende uitzonderingen:
> a. bestuurders op een onverharde weg verlenen voorrang aan bestuurders op een verharde weg;
> b. bestuurders verlenen voorrang aan bestuurders van een tram.

- "Kruispunt" = "kruising of splitsing van wegen" (RVV 1), so a **T-junction is a kruispunt** too.
- "Bestuurders" includes **cyclists, moped riders, snorfietsers, horse riders, brommobielen and trams**. So a **bike or moped from the right has priority over a car** at an unmarked junction, exactly as a car would. Pedestrians do not.
- A cycle path that belongs to a road shares that road's priority status. A cyclist on such a path coming from your right has priority at an unmarked junction **(interpretation from art. 15 + definition of bestuurder; RP-B p.27 lists "wegen met vrijliggende fiets- en/of voetpaden" as multiple conflict areas to clear)**.
- Exception (a): **unpaved road yields to paved road**, whatever the direction.
- Exception (b): **tram always has priority** at a junction, also when it comes from the left (unless signs or lights say otherwise; see 4.7).
- Turning traffic at an unmarked junction: art. 15 decides between crossing flows, and art. 18 decides between a turner and traffic on the same road. Both apply at once (see 4.5).
- Do not block the junction (RVV 14). Enter only if you can clear it (RP-B p.29).
- **Erven** use the same right-hand rule inside (RP-B p.77, "De voorrangsregeling binnen het erf is niet afwijkend").

### 4.2 Priority signs and haaientanden

Bijlage 1, Hoofdstuk B (RVV-B1):

| Sign | Official name | Meaning for priority |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | Voorrangsweg | You are on a priority road: crossing/joining traffic from side roads yields to you (they have B6/B7/haaientanden). |
| **B2** | Einde voorrangsweg | Priority road ends; general rules apply again. |
| **B3** | Voorrangskruispunt | At the next junction you have priority over both sides. |
| **B4** | Voorrangskruispunt, zijweg links | Next junction: side road from the left yields to you. |
| **B5** | Voorrangskruispunt, zijweg rechts | Next junction: side road from the right yields to you. |
| **B6** | "Verleen voorrang aan bestuurders op de kruisende weg" | Give way. |
| **B7** | "Stop; verleen voorrang aan bestuurders op de kruisende weg" | Stop (at the stop line, RVV 79), then give way. Without a stop line, stop where you can see the junction (RP-B p.31). |

(There is no "B8" in RVV Bijlage 1. B-signs run B1–B7.)

**Haaientanden**, definition (RVV 1): "voorrangsdriehoeken op het wegdek". **RVV 80**: "Haaientanden hebben de volgende betekenis: de bestuurders moeten voorrang verlenen aan bestuurders op de kruisende weg." Same legal effect as B6. They are a gebod/verbod road marking (BABW 6).

Key points for scenarios:
- Signs and markings override art. 15 (RVV 63). Under B6/haaientanden you also yield to traffic **from the left**, and to **cyclists and mopeds** on the crossing road and its cycle path. You still owe pedestrians nothing (only "bestuurders").
- **Between two road users who are both on the priority road**, or both on the yielding approaches, the general rules still decide among them: art. 18 for turning; art. 15 between two approaches that both have B6 facing a crossing road **(interpretation)**.
- **Afbuigende voorrang** (priority road bends at the junction; an onderbord under B1/B3 shows the shape): following the bent priority road **counts as going straight**. Leaving it counts as turning (RP-B p.27; signal anyway, RP-B p.26).
- **Set-back cycle path with its own haaientanden** (fietspad "afgeleid" from the main road) is a separate junction. The turning driver then has *no* art. 18 obligation towards those cyclists; the cyclists must give way (RP-B p.29).
- Haaientanden meant for crossing traffic are not lane guidance (RP-B p.30).
- B3/B4/B5/B6 on a 30 or 60 km/h road need a specific justification in the traffic decision (BABW 15). This is design trivia, not exam material.

### 4.3 Traffic lights

- **RVV 68(1)**, three-colour light: green = doorgaan. Yellow = stop, unless you are so close that you cannot reasonably stop, then go. Red = stop.
- **68(2) Arrows**: if a lit arrow is shown in a three-colour light (or in a one-colour light added to it), the light applies **only to the direction of the arrow**.
- 68(3) Bike symbol: the light applies to cyclists, moped riders on a fiets/bromfietspad and disabled-vehicle users.
- **68(5)** Onderbord "Rechtsaf voor (brom)fietsers vrij": yellow/red do not apply to right-turning (moped) cyclists, but they must let all other traffic go.
- 68(6) A light can apply to one lane only.
- **RVV 69** two-colour (yellow/red) lights, e.g. at a zebra or fire station. Same meaning as above, without green.
- **RVV 70** tram/bus lights (white = go). They apply to trams, lijnbussen and vehicles legally on that bus lane.
- **RVV 74** pedestrian lights. **74(2)**: if the red pedestrian light is replaced by a flashing yellow, pedestrians may cross but must let other traffic go.
- **RVV 75**: flashing yellow = "gevaarlijk punt; voorzichtigheid geboden". It gives no priority.
- **RVV 64**: lights beat priority signs. **Out of order or flashing yellow**: the lights no longer control priority, so the **signs and markings at the junction apply (B1–B7, haaientanden)**. If there are none, the **general rules apply (art. 15 right-hand rule, tram, unpaved road)**. This follows from arts. 63, 64 and 75 together **(interpretation; standard exam teaching)**. RP-B p.31 and p.53: extra care at flashing yellow.
- Green for two conflicting flows (e.g. a left turner and oncoming straight traffic both on green, no arrow): the light does not settle it. Art. 18 decides: the left turner lets oncoming traffic go **(interpretation from arts. 64/68/18)**.
- Right turn on green crossing a cycle path whose cyclists also have green: art. 18(1) applies, so let straight-ahead cyclists on the same road go **(interpretation)**.
- Railway crossing lights (RVV 71): flashing white = no train, flashing red = stop. Bridge lights (RVV 72). Lane signals (RVV 73).

### 4.4 Roundabouts

- **RVV has no article that gives circulating traffic priority.** Priority on a roundabout comes from **signs**: B6 and/or haaientanden on each approach make entering traffic yield (RVV 80, RVV-B1 B6). RP-B p.51–53 assumes B6 on the approach.
- A roundabout in the legal sense is marked with **sign D1** ("Rotonde; verplichte rijrichting"). Only on D1-roundabouts do the special RVV rules apply (RP-B p.51): **RVV 47** (cars and mopeds on the roadway may drive other than on the right side just before or on a roundabout) and **RVV 48** (overtaking on the right is allowed just before or on a roundabout). J9 is only a warning sign ("Rotonde").
- **Roundabout without priority signs**: the general rules apply, so art. 15 **right-hand rule**. Since you drive anticlockwise, traffic *entering* comes from the right of circulating traffic, so **entering traffic has priority over circulating traffic** **(interpretation from art. 15; RP-B p.51 tells you to check for "(brom)fietsverkeer dat van rechts nadert")**. This is an exam edge case.
- **Leaving the roundabout**: let traffic that stays on the roundabout go, "denk hierbij ook aan het verkeer op een vrijliggend fietspad" (RP-B p.52). The legal hook is art. 18(1) (turning off; the cycle path belongs to the same road) **(interpretation)**. If that circulating cycle path has its own haaientanden at the exit, the cyclists yield instead (same logic as RP-B p.29).
- Cyclists on a roundabout cycle path having or lacking priority is a **design choice made with signs and markings, not a rule**. The common design (cyclists have priority inside built-up areas, not outside) comes from CROW guidance, which I could not verify here. **Scenarios should always draw the haaientanden explicitly** rather than imply a convention.
- Lane choice, signalling and quarter/half/three-quarter rounds: RP-B p.52–54 (practical exam, minor for priority puzzles).

### 4.5 Turning conflicts

**RVV 18**
> 1 Bestuurders die afslaan, moeten het verkeer dat hen op dezelfde weg tegemoet komt of dat op dezelfde weg zich naast dan wel links of rechts dicht achter hen bevindt, voor laten gaan.
> 2 Bestuurders die naar links afslaan, moeten tegemoetkomende bestuurders die op hetzelfde kruispunt naar rechts afslaan voor laten gaan.
> 3 Het eerste en het tweede lid gelden niet voor bestuurders van een tram.

- **Left turn vs oncoming straight**: the left turner lets them go (18(1)).
- **Left turn vs oncoming right turn**: the left turner lets them go (18(2)).
- **Turning across a cycle path or lane on the same road** (straight-ahead cyclists or moped riders next to or just behind you): **right *and* left turners** let them go (18(1), "naast dan wel links of rechts dicht achter"). This holds even if the turner is on the priority road.
- **Pedestrians when turning**: art. 18 says "verkeer", and verkeer includes pedestrians. But CBR says "op kruispunten [hoeft] aan voetgangers wettelijk gezien geen voorrang ... te worden verleend". You should still take their interests into account (RP-B p.27). **Unresolved nuance**: model pedestrians crossing the side road as "let go for safety / sometimes expected" only where a zebra (art. 49) or lights apply, until verified (§6).
- **Two oncoming left turners**: preferably pass **in front of each other** ("voor elkaar langs"), unless road markings say otherwise (RP-B p.30). Neither has priority over the other under art. 18; they can move at the same time.
- Art. 18 runs alongside art. 15/signs. Classic exam chain at an unmarked junction: a car from the right has priority (15); a left turner must also let oncoming traffic go (18). The result can be a cycle → §5.
- Signalling and pre-sorting: RVV 17. Overtaking a left turner on the right: RVV 11(2).
- Trams are exempt from art. 18 (18(3)). A **turning tram does not have to let straight traffic go**, so you let "afbuigende trams" go (RP-B p.72).

### 4.6 Emergency vehicles

- **Voorrangsvoertuig** = "motorvoertuig dat de optische en geluidssignalen voert als bedoeld in artikel 29" (RVV 1). Art. 29(1): police, fire brigade, emergency medical services and designated rescue services carry **blue rotating, flashing or blinking light *and* a two-tone horn** when on an urgent task. Daytime flashing headlights are allowed in addition (29(2)).
- So **both blue light and siren are needed**. A vehicle with only blue lights, no siren, is *not* a voorrangsvoertuig, and the normal rules apply to it (definition RVV 1 + 29). This is a classic exam trap.
- **RVV 50**: all road users let a voorrangsvoertuig go. **RVV 91**: it may break RVV rules where its task requires (e.g. red light, wrong side).
- Yellow/green flashing lights (RVV 30) give **no** priority. Funeral processions and military columns may not be cut through (RVV 16). That is a separate "don't cut through" rule, not voorrang (art. 2(3) treats pedestrian processions like vehicles).
- Bus leaving a stop inside built-up area with its indicator on: let it go (RVV 56). This is not voorrang but is often bundled in exams.

### 4.7 Trams

- **Junctions**: tram has priority over all bestuurders, from either side (RVV 15(2)(b)).
- **Turning**: the tram is exempt from art. 18 (18(3)).
- Signs and lights still rank above this rule (RVV 63/64). A tram facing B6/haaientanden or a red/tram light must yield or stop. Tram lights: RVV 70 **(interpretation of 63 for B6; tram lights explicit in 70)**.
- You may overtake a tram on the right (RVV 11(5)). Passing a stopped tram or bus on the boarding side: give passengers the chance to get on and off (RVV 52).
- The J14 warning sign marks a tram crossing.

### 4.8 Uitritten (and other special manoeuvres)

**RVV 54**: drivers performing a special manoeuvre let **all other traffic** go. The listed manoeuvres are pulling away, reversing, driving out of an uitrit onto the road, turning off the road into an inrit, U-turning (keren), merging from an acceleration lane, moving onto an exit lane, and changing lanes.
- Because the duty is to let "het overige verkeer" go, it covers **pedestrians** on the pavement you cross, traffic from **both directions**, and oncoming traffic (RP-B p.44, p.77).
- **Uitrit is not defined in law.** Test (HOF, citing the RVV 1990 drafting history): can any road user **recognise it at a glance** ("in één oogopslag") as an in/uitrit? Purpose of the opening and its construction both count. Typical construction: pavement or cycle path continuing at roughly the same height and paving across the opening, and/or inritblokken (kerb ramps). A side street built this way is an uitrit, not a junction. The right-hand rule does not apply there.
- **Car leaving an uitrit and car entering the same in/uitrit**: both have a duty to let others go. CBR prefers that the one *leaving* goes first. On the practical exam only the *entering* driver is at fault (RP-B p.44, p.77). This is a candidate for a "simultaneous / either" scenario.
- Leaving a parking space = pulling away = special manoeuvre (RP-B p.19).

### 4.9 Erven

- Marked by **G5 (Erf)** and **G6 (Einde erf)** (RVV-B1). Not defined in RVV art. 1. The sign defines it.
- Inside: pedestrians may use the full width of the road (RVV 44). Max **15 km/h** (RVV 45). Park only in marked places (RVV 46).
- **Priority inside an erf: normal rules (right-hand rule etc.)** (RP-B p.77).
- **Leaving an erf**: no special RVV article. Erf exits usually have uitrit form, so art. 54 applies: you let everyone go. Some erf exits are a normal junction ("het karakter van een kruispunt"), and then art. 15 or signs apply (RP-B p.45). Older material says "leaving an erf = always yield". That rule is **not** in the current RVV. Scenarios must show the exit construction.

### 4.10 Pedestrians at zebra crossings

**RVV 49**
> 1 Bestuurders moeten blinden, voorzien van een witte stok met één of meer rode ringen, en overigens alle personen die zich moeilijk voortbewegen, voor laten gaan.
> 2 Bestuurders moeten voetgangers en bestuurders van een gehandicaptenvoertuig, die op een voetgangersoversteekplaats oversteken of kennelijk op het punt staan zulks te doen, voor laten gaan.
> 3 Het tweede lid geldt niet voor bestuurders van een motorvoertuig dat behoort tot een militaire colonne of een uitvaartstoet van motorvoertuigen.
> 4 Het tweede lid geldt evenmin, indien voor de voetgangers en de bestuurders van een gehandicaptenvoertuig een rood voetgangerslicht of een geel knipperlicht als bedoeld in artikel 74, tweede lid, van toepassing is.

- Zebra = voetgangersoversteekplaats, sign **L2**, warning sign **J22** (RVV-B1, RP-B p.48).
- "Kennelijk op het punt staan": at least people waiting to cross (RP-B p.49). You do not have to stop for someone far away on the other side (RP-B p.78).
- With a **flashing yellow** for pedestrians at the crossing (74(2)), pedestrians must let traffic go. Drivers should still expect them (RP-B p.18, p.49).
- No overtaking just before or on a zebra (RVV 12).
- Blind people with a white stick, and anyone who moves with difficulty: let them go **everywhere**, not only at zebras (49(1)).

### 4.11 Other "voor laten gaan" rules that show up in priority questions

- Railway crossing: let the train go and keep the crossing clear (RVV 15a).
- Bus pulling away from a stop inside built-up area (RVV 56).
- Merging from an acceleration lane (RVV 54). On a combined acceleration/exit lane, the merging driver lets the exiting driver go (RP-B p.35, p.74).
- Narrowing or obstacle on your side: no RVV article. RP-B p.75 treats letting oncoming traffic go as the norm. Sign F5/F6 settles who goes first ("F5: Verbod ... door te gaan bij nadering van verkeer uit tegengestelde richting"; F6 gives you priority over oncoming traffic) (RVV-B1).

## 5. Edge cases: deadlocks and simultaneity

- **Four-way deadlock** (four cars on an unmarked crossroads, each with someone from its right): **the RVV contains no tie-break.** Art. 15 creates a cycle. Nothing in RVV, BABW or CBR material names a winner. What official material does say:
  - Communicate: eye contact or hand gestures may be used to resolve it, and departing from the rule for flow or safety is acceptable (RP-B p.16). For narrow-road standoffs: "Zo nodig stoppen en in onderling overleg (bijvoorbeeld via oogcontact of handgebaren) de situatie oplossen" (RP-B p.22).
  - If a road user with priority **voluntarily** stops or brakes, you may use that gap (RP-B p.72).
  - Clear the junction "in volgorde van aankomst" (RP-B p.27). Do not block it (RVV 14).
  - **Model for the app (interpretation)**: a deadlock is a valid scenario outcome with no unique legal order. The correct answer is "one party waives priority by agreement", and any one road user may go first, after which the remaining order follows the rules. Treat as the optional advanced type, as the map already plans.
- **Three-way cycles with turners** (e.g. A from the right of B; B oncoming to left-turning C; C from the right of A) follow the same logic: art. 15 and art. 18 can conflict, and the resolution is waiver/communication **(interpretation)**.
- **Simultaneous moves** are legally fine when paths do not conflict (e.g. two oncoming right turners, two oncoming left turners passing in front of each other, straight traffic in opposite directions). The CBR sleepvraag wording suggests strict numbering (§1.3), so the app may need to accept either order for truly independent road users, or explain that CBR exam pictures avoid such ties. **Open question, see §6.**
- **Driving on the priority road vs a car from the right turning in**: the car on B1 still has priority over the B6 side road, whatever direction it turns.
- **Unpaved road from the right vs paved road**: the paved road wins (15(2)(a)).
- **Blue lights without siren**: not a voorrangsvoertuig (4.6).
- **Lights off vs flashing yellow**: both mean the lights do not control priority, so signs apply, else the general rules (4.3).

## 7. Suggested rules-engine ordering (for ticket 0003)

Built only from the articles above. For each pair of road users whose paths conflict:

1. Is one obeying an **aanwijzing**? Then the aanwijzing decides (84). *(Gestures themselves are out of scope per the map, but a "controller present" flag could exist.)*
2. Is one a **voorrangsvoertuig** (blue light **and** siren)? Then it goes first (50, 91).
3. Is one a **train** at a level crossing? Then it goes first (15a).
4. Are **lights** operating for these approaches? Then red/green decide which movements may enter (64, 68–70). Among movements that are both green, go to step 6.
5. Otherwise, do **signs/markings** (B1–B7, haaientanden, F5/F6) decide between them? Then they do (63, 80).
6. **Special manoeuvre** (uitrit, pulling away, U-turn, lane change)? The manoeuvring party lets all go, pedestrians included (54).
7. **Same road, one turning**: the turner lets go oncoming traffic and traffic next to or just behind it (incl. cyclists). A left turner also lets oncoming right turners go. A tram is exempt (18).
8. **Crossing approaches, no signs**: tram first, then paved over unpaved, then from the right (15).
9. **Pedestrians**: let go at a zebra unless red or flashing yellow for them (49(2), (4)), and in step 6. Blind / mobility-impaired people always (49(1)). Otherwise pedestrians yield.
10. If the resulting "must yield to" graph has a **cycle**, this is a deadlock: resolve by waiver (§5).
